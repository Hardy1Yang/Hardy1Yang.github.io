Dissertation

Precision and Robustness in Adaptive Testing: An Item Response Theory Analysis within an Online Convex Optimization Framework

Advisor: Prof. Yung-Fong Hsu · Completed: June 2026

Computerized adaptive testing (CAT) assigns items adaptively to estimate ability with as few items as possible. Despite a rich asymptotic literature, a framework for understanding finite-sample behavior has been missing. This dissertation brings the Online Convex Optimization (OCO) framework to CAT, organized around two complementary themes.

Precision
Shows that the classical information-maximization method is a no-regret algorithm under the OCO framework, and constructs anytime-valid confidence intervals via martingale concentration inequalities and confidence sequences.
Robustness
Designs stability-oriented item-selection algorithms — adapting Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) methods from the multi-armed bandit literature — that account for early-stage estimation error, premature commitment bias, and item-parameter uncertainty during online calibration.

Project Experience

  • On the Estimation of Standard Errors for the Ability Estimate in Item Response ModelsAug. 2024 – Present

    PI: Prof. Yung-Fong Hsu

    Concerns the estimation of standard errors for the ability estimate in IRT models, advancing estimation methods for computerized adaptive testing. My work covers algorithm development, simulation experiments, literature review, and manuscript preparation.

  • Family, Health, and the MarketJul. 2022 – Present

    PI: Prof. Kuan-Ming Chen

    Investigates interactions among individuals, families, and the market under health shocks and long-term care demands. I analyze government administrative data to study wage inequality in Taiwan with the Abowd–Kramarz–Margolis (AKM) framework, and intra-household decision-making and tax-filing strategies using comprehensive income-tax records, complemented by surveys and field experiments.

  • Evaluation of an Education Program Using Item Response TheoryJun. 2022 – Present

    PI: Prof. Yu-Chang Chen

    Evaluates the treatment effect of PaGamO, an online learning platform, using a 2-PL IRT model. I review the IRT literature, conduct algorithmic research, and implement the algorithms; we are developing a Bayesian state-space framework to estimate user ability and account for non-linear improvement over time.

  • Issue-Oriented Big Data Analyses on Social Inclusion and Smart GovernanceJul. 2023 – Jun. 2025

    PI: Prof. Kuan-Ming Chen

    Examines the substitutability/complementarity between infants and pets, using fertility decisions as a behavioral lens. The study links administrative data from Taiwan's Ministry of Finance (tax records) and Ministry of Agriculture (pet registration data).

  • Neural Correlates of Emotion as an Arbitrator to Reconcile the Conflicts within Dual Processing in the Context of Decision Making Under RiskMar. 2021 – Jan. 2023

    PI: Prof. Yung-Fong Hsu

    Behavioral and fMRI experiments probing the role of emotion when people face dual-process induced cognitive conflict under risk. I analyzed fMRI data and contributed to developing an estimation method for utility-function parameters.

  • Analysis of Cognitive Structures Underlying Financial BehaviorJun. 2020 – Nov. 2021

    PI: Prof. Tsung-Ren Huang

    Sought common factors explaining financial behavioral biases. I reviewed the bias-related literature and analyzed behavioral data using exploratory factor analysis.

  • From Mind Reading to Mind Sharing: Neural Correlates of Cognitive and Affective Theory of Mind and Their Applications to Salesforce EnhancementSep. 2018 – Jun. 2020

    PI: Prof. Heng-Chiang Huang

    Reviewed and reported literature on synchronization, structural equation modeling, and theory of mind, and mentored new team members in preprocessing and statistical analysis of behavioral and neuroimaging data.

  • Coalition without Trust: Intra-Brain Connectivity and Inter-Brain Synchronization of Herd Behaviors in an Economic Bubble GameSep. 2017 – Jun. 2020

    PI: Prof. Yu-Ping Chen

    Used fMRI hyperscanning to explore theory-of-mind processes when participants competed in a bubble stock-market game. I reviewed literature spanning the diffusion model, reinforcement learning, prospect theory, and behavioral economics, and analyzed the fMRI and behavioral data.
